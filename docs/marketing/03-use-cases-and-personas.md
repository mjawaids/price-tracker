# SpendLess — Use Cases & Personas

Use these for use-case landing sections, ad targeting, and shaping testimonials.

## Personas

### 1. "Budget Bushra" — the household optimizer
- Runs the weekly grocery shop for a family of four in a city with high inflation.
- Already knows "atta is cheaper at the wholesale store, but milk is cheaper at the mart."
- **Job:** Cut the monthly grocery bill without spending hours comparing.
- **Win:** Records prices once, then every weekly cart gets an optimized split.

### 2. "Online-first Omar" — the delivery juggler
- Orders groceries from two or three online stores; hates paying multiple delivery fees.
- Always wonders whether to consolidate into one order to hit the free-delivery threshold.
- **Job:** Minimize total spend including delivery, across online stores.
- **Win:** The optimizer tells him exactly which split (or which single store) is cheapest.

### 3. "Deal-tracker Dan" — the spreadsheet refugee
- Keeps a messy notes/spreadsheet of "X is cheaper at Y."
- **Job:** Turn scattered price notes into something that actually saves money.
- **Win:** A clean, searchable price book that does the optimization for him.

### 4. "New-city Nadia" — the relocator
- Just moved (possibly to a new country/currency) and doesn't know the cheapest stores.
- **Job:** Build price intuition for a new area, in the local currency.
- **Win:** Auto-detected currency + a personal catalog she builds as she learns the area.

### 5. "Small-business Sami" — the reseller / caterer
- Buys recurring supplies and wants the cheapest sourcing split each time.
- **Job:** Lower input costs on repeat purchases.
- **Win:** Saved catalog of suppliers/prices and an optimized buy plan per order.

## Concrete use cases (each can be its own landing section or blog post)

1. **The weekly grocery run.** Build your cart, get the cheapest store split, shop once.
2. **Hitting the free-delivery threshold.** Should you add items to one online order to
   cross "free over $X," or split? SpendLess answers with the cheaper total.
3. **One big store vs. several cheaper ones.** See whether convenience (one stop, one
   delivery) actually costs more than splitting — in real numbers.
4. **Stocking up on staples.** Track rice, oil, atta, sugar across wholesalers and marts;
   buy each where it's genuinely cheapest once delivery is counted.
5. **Comparing a new store.** Add a new store's prices and instantly see if it changes
   your optimal plan.
6. **Currency-correct shopping while traveling or after relocating.** Auto-detected
   currency keeps prices sensible without setup.
7. **Family budgeting.** Keep a shared mental model of "where things are cheapest" in one
   private, organized place.

## Benefit framing (outcomes, not features)
- "Spend less on the exact same groceries."
- "Stop paying three delivery fees when one order would've been cheaper."
- "Know — not guess — the cheapest place to buy each thing."
- "Turn 'I think it's cheaper there' into a real plan."
- "A weekly shop that optimizes itself once you've set it up."

## Objection-handling (for FAQ / sales copy)
- *"Doesn't this take effort to set up?"* — You add prices once; every future cart reuses
  them. The payoff compounds on repeat shops.
- *"Why not a price-comparison website?"* — Those use a fixed set of big retailers and
  ignore delivery. SpendLess uses **your** stores (including local shops) and optimizes the
  **whole cart including delivery** — where the real savings are.
- *"Is my data safe?"* — Data is private to your account, isolated with row-level security,
  with no ads and no data selling.
- *"Do I need to install anything?"* — No app store needed. Open it in a browser and, if you
  like, add it to your home screen to run it like a native app.
